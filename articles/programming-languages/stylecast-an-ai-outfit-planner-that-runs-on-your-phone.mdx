# StyleCast: an AI outfit planner that runs on your phone

## Introduction
Choosing what to wear each morning is a surprisingly complex decision. You have to consider the weather, your schedule, personal taste, and the limited set of clothes you actually own. Doing this manually can eat up precious minutes and lead to decision fatigue. StyleCast tackles the problem by moving a lightweight recommendation model onto the device, so you get instant, private suggestions without sending photos to a server.

## Why This Matters
For software engineers, StyleCast is a concrete case study in edge AI. It shows how to balance model accuracy with strict latency and power budgets on a mobile CPU/GPU. The pattern also highlights language‑specific choices—Swift on iOS, Kotlin on Android, or Rust for cross‑platform cores—and demonstrates how to integrate TensorFlow Lite (or ONNX Runtime) with platform‑specific delegates. Understanding these trade‑offs helps you build any on‑device intelligence feature, from voice commands to augmented‑reality filters.

## How It Works
At a high level StyleCast follows four stages:

1. **Data ingestion** – load the user’s wardrobe from a local SQLite/Realm store and pull contextual signals (weather API, calendar events).  
2. **Feature encoding** – each clothing item is turned into a fixed‑length vector that captures its visual attributes (color histogram, texture embeddings) and how it matches the current context (e.g., “formal” vs. “casual”).  
3. **Model inference** – a tiny fully‑connected network scores complete outfits (top + bottom + shoes). The network runs with TensorFlow Lite, optionally delegated to the phone’s GPU or NPU.  
4. **Ranking & presentation** – the top‑k outfits are sorted by score and shown in a simple UI.

**Step‑by‑step breakdown**

* **Wardrobe loading** – we keep a thin wrapper around SQLite that returns `[ClothingItem]` objects. The read is performed on a background `DispatchQueue` (iOS) or `Coroutine` (Android) to avoid blocking the main thread.  
* **Context acquisition** – a lightweight networking layer calls a free weather endpoint (e.g., Open‑Meteo) and reads the user's calendar via EventKit/CalendarContract. Results are cached for 15 minutes to reduce radio wake‑ups.  
* **Feature encoding** – each item yields a 128‑dim vector: the first 64 dimensions come from a pretrained MobileNetV3 feature extractor (run once per item and stored), the next 32 dimensions are a normalized histogram of HSV colors, and the final 32 dimensions are a one‑hot encoding of the context (e.g., `[isRainy, isMeeting, isWeekend]`). All vectors are L2‑normalized before being fed to the network.
