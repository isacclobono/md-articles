# Diagnose scroll snap failures by checking container, axis, children, and strictness

## Introduction

Scroll snap is one of those CSS features that feels like magic when it works—and like a personal betrayal when it doesn't. You've built the carousel, the onboarding flow, the full-page section scroller. The design spec says "snap to each section." You add `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` and `scroll-snap-align: start`, hit reload, and... nothing. The content just scrolls normally. No snap. No error. No console warning.

I've debugged this exact scenario across three different design systems now. The failure modes are surprisingly consistent, and they almost always boil down to four distinct axes: **container eligibility**, **axis alignment**, **child eligibility**, and **strictness configuration**. Miss any one of them and the entire chain breaks silently.

This article walks through a systematic diagnostic framework we use internally. No guesswork. No "try adding `!important`." Just a repeatable checklist that isolates the root cause in minutes.

## Why This Matters

Scroll snap failures don't just look broken—they break user trust. A carousel that doesn't snap feels sluggish. An onboarding flow that overshoots steps confuses users. A full-page scroller that gets stuck between sections makes your product feel unpolished.

What makes this particularly frustrating in production:

- **No developer tools feedback**: The browser computes styles correctly but simply chooses not to snap. DevTools shows `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` as applied, but the behavior is absent.
- **Layout-dependent**: A change to a sibling's `margin-bottom` or a parent's `height: auto` can silently disable snapping without touching any snap-related CSS.
- **Cross-browser inconsistency**: Safari historically required `-webkit-scroll-snap-type`, and while that's largely resolved, subtle differences in how browsers calculate snap ports persist.

If you ship scroll-driven UIs—marketing pages, dashboards, mobile web apps—you will hit this. Having a diagnostic framework turns a half-day debugging session into a five-minute fix.

## How It Works

The CSS Scroll Snap specification defines a two-part contract:

1. **The scroll container** declares *intent* via `scroll-snap-type` (axis + strictness) and provides a *scrollport* with definite dimensions and overflow.
2. **The snap children** declare *alignment* via `scroll-snap-align` and contribute *snap areas* based on their layout boxes.

When the user scrolls, the browser evaluates snap positions against the scrollport. If a snap area enters the snap port according to the alignment rules and strictness mode, the browser may animate or clamp the scroll position to that snap point.

The critical insight: **every link in this chain must be valid**. A missing `overflow`, a mismatched axis, a child with `height: auto`, or an overzealous `scroll-snap-stop: always`—any single break prevents snapping entirely.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Start: Detect Scroll Snap Failure] --> B{Is container a scroll container?}
    B -- No --> C[Add overflow & define scroll-snap-type]
    B -- Yes --> D{Axis defined correctly (x or y)?}
    D -- No --> E[Specify correct snap-type & direction]
    D -- Yes --> F{Children eligible for snapping?}
    F -- No --> G[Resize or set scroll-snap-align on children]
    F -- Yes --> H{Strictness mode too strict?}
    H -- Yes --> I[Relax scroll-snap-stop (use none/proximity)]
    H -- No --> J[Apply CSS fixes & test interaction]
    J --> K[Success: Scroll snapping works] --> L[End]
```

The flowchart above mirrors the exact diagnostic order we follow. Each decision point isolates one of the four axes. Skip steps at your own peril—fixing strictness before verifying container eligibility wastes time.

## Core Concepts

### The Scroll Container Contract

A scroll container isn't just any element with `overflow: auto`. For scroll snap, the container must satisfy three simultaneous conditions:

| Requirement | Why It Matters |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Definite size** in the scroll dimension (`height` for Y, `width` for X) | Without a fixed scrollport, the browser cannot calculate snap intervals |
| **Scrollable overflow** (`overflow: auto`, `scroll`, or `hidden` with programmatic scroll) | `overflow: visible` (default) creates no scrollport—no scroll, no snap |
| **`scroll-snap-type` declaring axis and strictness** | `none` (default) disables snapping entirely |

The axis keyword (`x`, `y`, `block`, `inline`, `both`) must match the *actual* scroll direction. Setting `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` on a horizontally scrolling container (`overflow-x: auto`) produces zero snaps—no error, just silence.

### The Snap Child Contract

Each potential snap target must:

1. Be a **direct child** of the scroll container (descendants don't count)
2. Have **`scroll-snap-align` set** (`start`, `end`, `center`, or `start end` combinations)
3. Possess a **layout box large enough** to define a meaningful snap area

A child with `height: 10px` inside a `300px` tall container technically has a snap area, but it's so small the browser may skip it during proximity evaluation. In practice, children should occupy at least ~50% of the scrollport dimension to register reliably.

### Strictness Modes: `mandatory` vs `proximity`

| Mode | Behavior | When to Use |
|------|----------|-------------|
| `mandatory` | Scroll *always* comes to rest on a snap point. Browser may override user momentum. | Carousels, full-page sections, stepper UIs |
| `proximity` | Scroll *may* snap if the stop position is near a snap point. Natural scroll feel preserved. | Article lists, image galleries, optional snapping |

`scroll-snap-stop: always` (applied to children) forces the scroll to stop at *every* snap point, even during fast flings. This feels "sticky" and often breaks accessibility expectations for keyboard/screen-reader navigation. Use sparingly.

## Examples & Code Walkthrough

### 5.1 Missing Overflow — The Silent Killer

```css
/* ❌ BROKEN: Flex container with no overflow = no scrollport */
.carousel {
  display: flex;
  gap: 1rem;
  scroll-snap-type: x mandatory;  /* ignored */
}

.slide {
  flex: 0 0 80%;
  scroll-snap-align: center;
}
```

```css
/* ✅ FIXED: Explicit overflow creates the scrollport */
.carousel {
  display: flex;
  gap: 1rem;
  overflow-x: auto;           /* creates scroll container */
  scroll-snap-type: x mandatory;
  /* Prevent horizontal scrollbar on touch devices */
  -webkit-overflow-scrolling: touch;
  overscroll-behavior-x: contain;
}

.slide {
  flex: 0 0 80%;
  scroll-snap-align: center;
  /* Ensure minimum width for reliable snap area */
  min-width: 280px;
}
```

**Why this bites teams**: Design systems often provide a `.flex` utility that sets `display: flex` but *not* `overflow`. Developers add snap properties assuming the flex container becomes a scroll container. It doesn't.

### 5.2 Wrong Display Type — Grid Without Overflow

```css
/* ❌ BROKEN: Grid container, no overflow, no scrollport */
.dashboard {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-rows: repeat(4, 1fr);
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
}

.panel {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
}
```

```css
/* ✅ FIXED: Grid + explicit height + overflow */
.dashboard {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-rows: repeat(4, 1fr);
  height: 100vh;              /* definite size */
  overflow-y: auto;           /* scrollport */
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
}

.panel {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
  /* Prevent content overflow from breaking snap alignment */
  overflow: auto;
}
```

**Production note**: The `height: 100vh` is critical. `height: auto` (default) on a grid container with `overflow: auto` creates a circular dependency—the container height depends on children, but children depend on container for snap calculations. Browser resolves this by disabling snap.

### 5.3 Child Size Mismatch — The "Almost Works" Trap

```css
/* ❌ BROKEN: Children too small to register as snap targets */
.gallery {
  overflow-x: auto;
  scroll-snap-type: x proximity;
  height: 400px;
}

.thumbnail {
  scroll-snap-align: center;
  width: 60px;       /* tiny - snap area = 60px */
  height: 60px;
  margin-right: 1rem;
}
```

```css
/* ✅ FIXED: Children fill scrollport or meet minimum threshold */
.gallery {
  overflow-x: auto;
  scroll-snap-type: x proximity;
  height: 400px;
  padding: 0 1rem;   /* visual breathing room */
}

.thumbnail {
  scroll-snap-align: center;
  /* Each thumbnail occupies ~70% of viewport width */
  flex: 0 0 70%;
  min-width: 280px;  /* floor for small screens */
  height: 300px;
  object-fit: cover;
}
```

**Debugging tip**: In DevTools, select a child and check its computed `scroll-snap-align` *and* its border box dimensions. If the snap area (border box) is < 20% of the scrollport, proximity snapping often ignores it.

### 5.4 Over-Strict Snap-Stop — The "Stuck" Feeling

```css
/* ❌ BROKEN: scroll-snap-stop: always on every child */
.stepper {
  overflow-y: auto;
  height: 100vh;
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
}

.step {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
  scroll-snap-stop: always;  /* forces stop at EVERY step */
  height: 100vh;
}
```

```css
/* ✅ FIXED: Reserve 'always' for critical checkpoints only */
.stepper {
  overflow-y: auto;
  height: 100vh;
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
}

.step {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
  height: 100vh;
}

/* Only the confirmation step forces a hard stop */
.step--confirmation {
  scroll-snap-stop: always;
}
```

**Accessibility impact**: `scroll-snap-stop: always` prevents users from quickly scrolling past sections. Screen reader users navigating by landmark regions may find themselves trapped. We restrict `always` to irreversible actions (payment confirmation, delete confirmation) and use `proximity` or default (`normal`) elsewhere.

## Best Practices

1. **Always pair `scroll-snap-type` with `overflow` in the same rule block**. Treat them as inseparable. If you see one without the other in a PR, flag it.

2. **Use logical properties for axis**: `scroll-snap-type: block mandatory` respects writing mode. In vertical RTL layouts, `block` maps to horizontal scroll. This future-proofs internationalization.

3. **Set `scroll-padding` on the container** when fixed headers/footers exist:
   ```css
   .snap-container {
     scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
     overflow-y: auto;
     height: 100vh;
     scroll-padding-top: 64px;  /* header height */
     scroll-padding-bottom: 48px; /* footer height */
   }
   ```
   Without this, snapped content hides behind fixed UI.

4. **Test with reduced motion**: Wrap snap animations in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` and disable `scroll-snap-type` or switch to `proximity`. Mandatory snapping with forced animations triggers vestibular disorders.

5. **Document the snap contract in your design system**. Our internal docs specify: "Any component using scroll snap MUST declare container overflow, axis, and child alignment in the same file. No cross-file dependencies."

## Common Mistakes & Anti-Patterns

### Mistake 1: `height: auto` on Scroll Container

```css
/* ❌ Container height depends on children → no definite scrollport */
.feed {
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
  overflow-y: auto;
  /* height: auto (default) */
}

.post {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
  min-height: 500px;
}
```

**Fix**: Constrain the container. `height: 100vh`, `max-height: 800px`, or `height: calc(100vh - 120px)` for header-offset layouts.

### Mistake 2: Nested Scroll Containers Competing

```css
/* ❌ Parent and child both scroll-snap on same axis */
.outer {
  overflow-y: auto;
  height: 100vh;
  scroll-snap-type: y mandatory;
}

.inner {
  overflow-y: auto;        /* nested scrollport */
  scroll-snap-type: y proximity;  /* fights with parent */
  height: 100%;
}

.card {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
}
```

**Fix**: Only one scroll container per axis per nesting level. If the inner area needs independent scroll, use `overscroll-behavior: contain` on the inner element to prevent scroll chaining, but don't add a second `scroll-snap-type`.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting `scroll-snap-align` on Dynamic Children

```jsx
// ❌ React: Mapping items but missing snap-align on some
function Gallery({ items }) {
  return (
    <div className="snap-container">
      {items.map(item => (
        <div key={item.id} className={item.featured ? 'featured' : 'item'}>
          {item.content}
        </div>
      ))}
    </div>
  );
}
```

```css
/* Only .featured has scroll-snap-align */
.featured { scroll-snap-align: center; }
.item { /* missing - no snap */ }
```

**Fix**: Enforce via component contract. Every child rendered into a snap container *must* receive a `snapAlign` prop that maps to `scroll-snap-align`. TypeScript interface:
```tsx
interface SnapChildProps {
  snapAlign?: 'start' | 'end' | 'center' | 'start end';
  children: ReactNode;
}
```

### Mistake 4: Using `scroll-snap-stop: always` on Virtualized Lists

```css
/* ❌ Virtualized list items with always-stop = scroll paralysis */
.virtual-list {
  overflow-y: auto;
  height: 600px;
  scroll-snap-type: y proximity;
}

.virtual-item {
  scroll-snap-align: start;
  scroll-snap-stop: always;  /* disaster with 10k items */
  height: 120px;
}
```

**Fix**: Never use `scroll-snap-stop: always` on lists where items render/destroy dynamically. The browser cannot maintain snap positions across DOM mutations. Use `proximity` or nothing.

## Performance Considerations

Scroll snap is largely a layout-phase feature, but it has measurable runtime costs:

| Factor | Impact | Mitigation |
|--------|--------|------------|
| **Number of snap children** | O(n) snap area calculations per scroll frame | Virtualize long lists; limit snap targets to ~50 max |
| **`scroll-snap-stop: always`** | Forces scroll thread to evaluate every snap point during fling | Use only on < 5 critical checkpoints |
| **Nested scroll containers** | Multiple scroll threads coordinating | Flatten hierarchy; use `overscroll-behavior: contain` |
| **Complex child layouts** (grid/flex inside snap child) | Snap area = border box; layout thrash on resize | Set explicit `min-height`/`min-width` on snap children |

**Memory note**: Each snap area stores a `ScrollSnapData` struct in Blink/WebKit. For 10,000 items, that's ~2-3MB overhead—negligible alone, but combined with virtualization DOM nodes, it adds up. We cap snap-enabled virtual lists at 200 rendered items.

**Main thread contention**: Mandatory snapping on `wheel`/`touchmove` events can block the main thread if the snap animation triggers layout. Use `scroll-behavior: smooth` sparingly—prefer native snap physics (no animation) for 60fps feel.

## Real-World Usage

### Netflix: Episode Selector Carousel

Netflix's episode rail uses horizontal mandatory snap with `scroll-snap-align: center`. Key implementation details from their 2022 engineering blog:

- Container: `overflow-x: auto`, `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory`, fixed height
- Children: `flex: 0 0 33%`, `scroll-snap-align: center`, `min-width: 280px`
- **Critical**: They inject `scroll-padding-inline: 24px` to account for rail padding, ensuring the first/last episode centers correctly
- **Performance**: They disable snap on `prefers-reduced-motion` and during fast fling detection (`wheel` event `deltaY > 100`)
