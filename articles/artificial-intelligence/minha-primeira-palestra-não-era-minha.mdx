# Minha primeira palestra não era minha

## Introduction

Last month, I received a draft for a talk I'd agreed to give at a regional developer conference. The grammar was flawless. The structure followed every keynote convention: hook, three pillars, call to action. It was, by every measurable metric, a good talk.

It was also completely hollow.

The humor landed in all the wrong places. The metaphors were borrowed from someone else's engineering journey. When I read it aloud, I heard a stranger's voice—polished, competent, and utterly forgettable. That draft wasn't an extension of my thinking. It was a statistical average of what the model thought a software engineer who'd given talks might say.

This isn't a story about one bad prompt. It's a story about an architectural gap that the entire AI content industry is ignoring: the difference between generating text and amplifying a voice.

## Why This Matters

Every week, another developer posts a blog post written entirely by a large language model. Every month, a startup ships a "personalized AI assistant" that sounds like everyone and no one. The production pain point isn't hallucination or factual accuracy—those are solvable problems. The real problem is identity collapse.

When you feed a developer's past talks, blog posts, and Slack messages into an embedding pipeline and the output still sounds generic, you've built a system that processes context but ignores intent. You've created a mirror that reflects the average of everything it's seen instead of the unique shape of the person standing in front of it.

For engineers building AI-powered content tools, this matters because your users will eventually notice. They'll upload their entire GitHub history and get back prose that could have been written by anyone. Retention drops. Trust erodes. And the architecture you built—the one without a feedback loop, without a voice model, without intent mapping—becomes technical debt that compounds with every new user.

## How It Works

The architecture I'm describing treats a person's voice as a first-class data structure, not a prompt engineering trick. Here's how the pipeline flows:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Historical Talks & Writings] --> B[Voice Fingerprint Module]
    B --> C{Style Embeddings}
    C --> D[Intent Engine]
    E[Topic & Audience Input] --> D
    D --> F[Draft Generator]松弛
    F --> G[Speaker Review Loop]
    G --> H{Revisions Needed?}
    H -- Yes --> I[Update Style Weights]
    I --> C
    H -- No --> J[Final Talk Output]
```

The pipeline has four distinct stages. First, the **Voice Fingerprint Module** ingests everything a person has ever written publicly—conference recordings transcribed to text, blog posts, even code comments if they're expressive enough. It doesn't just tokenize; it extracts rhythmic patterns, recurring metaphor structures, and emotional arcs across documents.

Second, those extracted patterns become **Style Embeddings**—dense vector representations that capture not just what someone writes, but *how* they write. A developer who always uses infrastructure analogies when explaining distributed systems gets encoded differently than one who reaches for cooking metaphors.

Third, the **Intent Engine** takes the topic and audience profile and constrains the generation space. This is where most systems fail. They treat topic and voice as independent variables. In reality, what you say about Kubernetes is filtered through *who you are* when you say it. The Intent Engine maps the intersection.

Fourth, the **Speaker Review Loop** closes the system. Every edit the speaker makes during drafting retrains the style weight vectors. This is the feedback mechanism that turns a static generator into a living system. Without it, you're just running the same prompt through the same model and hoping for different results.

## Core Concepts

Three concepts anchor this architecture:

**Voice as a Vector Space.** Your linguistic DNA isn't a single feature—it's a multidimensional space. Sentence length distribution, preference for active versus passive voice, frequency of hedging language ("I think," "it seems"), and metaphor density all form coordinates in this space. When we extract a voice fingerprint, we're projecting a high-dimensional personality onto a manageable set of axes.

**Intent as a Constraint Graph.** Unlike a simple topic tag, intent captures the *purpose* behind a piece of content. Are you teaching? Persuading? Entertaining? A talk about distributed systems meant for junior developers carries entirely different constraints than the same topic aimed at staff engineers. The intent graph encodes these constraints as weighted edges that modulate the generation process.

**Feedback as Gradient Descent.** Every revision a speaker makes is a gradient signal. If the model generates a joke and the speaker replaces it with a technical analogy, the style weights shift. We're not doing full retraining—we're doing fine-grained adjustments to the embedding space that gradually pull the model's output closer to the speaker's authentic voice.

import re
from collections import Counter
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import List, Dict, Optional
import math


@dataclass
class VoiceFingerprint:
    """Stores the extracted linguistic DNA from a speaker's corpus."""
    avg_sentence_length: float
    metaphor_density: float
    emotional_arc: List[float]
    preferred_connectors: Counter
    hedging_frequency: float
    rhetorical_question_rate: float
    vocabulary_richness: float
    signature_phrases: List[str]


class VoiceAnalyzer:
    """
    Extracts a speaker's voice fingerprint from their historical writings.
    
    Processes a corpus of text documents and produces a VoiceFingerprint
    object containing measurable linguistic characteristics.
    """

    # Common hedging words that indicate uncertainty in speech
    HEDGING_WORDS = {
        'i think', 'i believe', 'it seems', 'i guess', 'perhaps',
        'maybe', 'could be', 'might be', 'sort of', 'kind of',
        'i feel like', 'i would say', 'i suppose'
    }

    # Connectors that reveal discourse patterns
    DISCourse_CONNECTORS = {
        'however', 'therefore', 'consequently', 'meanwhile',
        'furthermore', 'nevertheless', 'specifically', 'for instance'
    }

    def __init__(self, corpus: List[str]):
        """
        Initialize with a list of text documents.
        
        Args:
            corpus: List of strings, each representing a document
                    (talk transcript, blog post, etc.)
        """
        if not corpus or not all(isinstance(doc, str) for doc in corpus):
            raise ValueError("Corpus must be a non-empty list of strings")
        
        self.corpus = corpus
        self.tokenized_docs = [self._tokenize(doc) for doc in corpus]
        self.fingerprint: Optional[VoiceFingerprint] = None

    def _tokenize(self, text: str) -> List[str]:
        """
        Splits text into sentences and normalizes whitespace.
        
        Handles edge cases: multiple spaces, trailing punctuation,
        and empty sentences from aggressive splitting.
        """
        # Split on sentence boundaries while preserving structure
        sentences = re.split(r'[.!?]+', text)
        # Filter out empty strings and strip whitespace
        return [s.strip().lower() for s in sentences if s.strip()]

    def _calculate_sentence_length_stats(self) -> float:
        """Computes average tokens per sentence across the corpus."""
        all_lengths = []
        for doc_tokens in self.tokenized_docs:
            for sentence in doc_tokens:
                word_count = len(sentence.split())
                if word_count > 0:
                    all_lengths.append(word_count)
        
        if not all_lengths:
            return 0.0
        return sum(all_lengths) / len(all_lengths)

    def _detect_metaphors(self) -> float:
        """
        Estimates metaphor density by identifying non-literal language patterns.
        
        Looks for common engineering metaphor structures:
        - 'pipeline' used as verb
        - 'layer' referring to abstraction levels
        - 'bridge' used figuratively
        """
        metaphor_indicators = [
            'pipeline', 'layer', 'bridge', 'fabric', 'backbone',
            'heartbeat', 'roots', 'prune', 'fork', 'sandbox'
        ]
        
        total_words = 0
        metaphor_count = 0
        
        for doc_tokens in self.tokenized_docs:
            for sentence in doc_tokens:
                words = sentence.split()
                total_words += len(words)
                for word in words:
                    if word in metaphor_indicators:
                        metaphor_count += 1
        
        if total_words == 0:
            return 0.0
        return metaphor_count / total_words

    def _map_emotional_arc(self) -> List[float]:
        """
        Creates a sentiment trajectory across the corpus.
        
        Returns a list of sentiment scores (simplified: -1.0 to 1.0)
        representing the emotional tone of each document in order.
        """
        # Simplified sentiment: count positive vs negative indicators
        positive_indicators = {'love', 'great', 'amazing', 'excited', 'best', 'beautiful'}
        negative_indicators = {'hate', 'terrible', 'broken', 'worst', 'frustrated', 'pain'}
        
        arc = []
        for doc_tokens in self.tokenized_docs:
            text = ' '.join(doc_tokens)
            pos_count = sum(1 for word in text.split() if word in positive_indicators)
            neg_count = sum(1 for word in text.split() if word in negative_indicators)
            
            total = max(pos_count + neg_count, 1)  # Avoid division by zero
            score = (pos_count - neg_count) / total
            arc.append(round(score, 3))
        
        return arc

    def _find_signature_phrases(self, top_n: int = 5) -> List[str]:
        """
        Identifies multi-word phrases that appear disproportionately
        in this speaker's corpus compared to general usage.
        """
        phrase_counter = Counter()
        
        for doc_tokens in self.tokenized_docs:
            for sentence in doc_tokens:
                words = sentence.split()
                # Extract 2-3 word phrases
                for i in range(len(words) - 1):
                    phrase = ' '.join(words[i:i+2])
                    if len(phrase.split()) >= 2:
                        phrase_counter[phrase] += 1
        
        # Return the most frequent unique phrases
        return [phrase for phrase, _ in phrase_counter.most_common(top_n)]

    def extract_fingerprint(self) -> VoiceFingerprint:
        """
        Orchestrates all analysis steps and returns a complete fingerprint.
        
        This is the main entry point. Call this once after initialization;
        the result is cached in self.fingerprint.
        """
        avg_length = self._calculate_sentence_length_stats()
        metaphor_density = self._detect_metaphors()
        emotional_arc = self._map_emotional_arc()
        signature_phrases = self._find_signature_phrases()

        # Count discourse connectors and hedging words
        connector_counts = Counter()
        hedging_count = 0
        total_sentences = 0
        rhetorical_questions = 0

        for doc_tokens in self.tokenized_docs:
            for sentence in doc_tokens:
                total_sentences += 1
                words = sentence.split()
                word_set = set(words)
                
                for connector in self.DISCOURSE_CONNECTORS:
                    if connector in word_set:
                        connector_counts[connector] += 1
                
                for hedge in self.HEDGING_WORDS:
                    if hedge in sentence:
                        hedging_count += 1
                
                # Detect rhetorical questions (questions without question words
                # that expect agreement or are used for emphasis)
                if sentence.endswith('?') and not any(
                    wh in sentence for wh in ['who', 'what', 'where', 'when', 'why', 'how']
                ):
                    rhetorical_questions += 1

        # Calculate derived metrics
        hedging_freq = hedging_count / max(total_sentences, 1)
        vocab_richness = len(set(
            word for doc in self.tokenized_docs for sentence in doc for word in sentence.split()
        )) / max(sum(len(s.split()) for s in self.tokenized_docs), 1)

        self.fingerprint = VoiceFingerprint(
            avg_sentence_length=round(avg_length, 2),
            metaphor_density=round(metaphor_density, 4),
            emotional_arc=emotional_arc,
            preferred_connectors=connector_counts,
            hedging_frequency=round(hedging_freq, 4),
            rhetorical_question_rate=round(rhetorical_questions / max(total_sentences, 1), 4),
            vocabulary_richness=round(vocab_richness, 4),
            signature_phrases=signature_phrases
        )

        return self.fingerprint
